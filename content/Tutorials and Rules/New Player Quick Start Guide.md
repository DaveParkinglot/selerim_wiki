Every game of D&D is different, but every game of D&D follows this basic loop:
![[gameplay_loops.png|800]]
90% of D&D is describing what your character does in a situation. The other 10% is rolling dice.

## Combat
Most rules in D&D are for handling combat situations. In combat, players take turns like in a board game, based on their Initiative roll.

On your turn, you have 3 resources that you can spend: **Movement**, **Action**, **Bonus Action**. You can use these in any order.

### Movement
Move up to your character's Speed. Most characters have a Speed of 30 feet. On a hex or square grid, each space is 5 feet.

### 1 Action
You always get 1 Action per turn. Some characters like to use their weapons to take the Attack action, while others love casting spells using the Magic action.

### 1 Bonus Action
You only can take a Bonus Action if your character has an ability that costs a Bonus Action. The fighter's Second Wind feature is an example of a Bonus Action ability.

### Example Turn - Joe the Fighter
1. Joe uses 10 feet of **MOVEMENT** to get in range of the evil orc.
2. Joe uses his **ACTION** to attack the evil orc with his Shortbow.
3. Joe uses his remaining 20 feet of **MOVEMENT** to move away from the orc.
4. Joe uses his **BONUS ACTION** to activate his Second Wind fighter feature, replenishing his health.

| **Magic**     | Cast a spell, use a magic item, or use a magical feature.                                                                                                                                                               |

### Reactions
During each round of combat, you also have one other resource: the **Reaction**.

Reactions occur off of your turn, and are uncommon. Generally your character will only use a Reaction if:
- A creature provokes an Opportunity Attack against you. This happens if an enemy moves out of your reach in combat.
- You have a feature that lets you use a Reaction (e.g., the Shield spell can be cast as a Reaction, in response to being attacked).

## Out of Combat
Out of combat, the game does not follow a specific structure. Listen to your DM, ask questions, and collaborate with your fellow players to overcome challenges.

The rest is up to you and your group, but these 2 guidelines can be helpful for new players:
- Be a team player, and share the spotlight with the other players at your table.
- Don't get bogged down by making optimal choices—D&D isn't a game about winning.
