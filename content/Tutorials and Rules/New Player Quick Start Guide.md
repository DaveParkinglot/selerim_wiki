## Rhythm of Play

Every game of D\&D is different, but every game of D\&D follows this basic pattern:

1. **The Dungeon Master Describes a Scene.**
2. **The Players Describe What Their Characters Do.**
3. **The DM Narrates the Results of the Adventurers' Actions.**

While it helps to know what your character's options are, describing what your character does in a situation is 90% of what D\&D is. The other 10% is rolling dice.

## Combat Rules

Most rules in D\&D are for handling combat situations. In combat, players take turns like in a board game based on their Initiative roll.

On your turn, you have 3 resources that you can spend. You can use these in any order. Your character sheet tells you what type of resource an ability requires.

#### Movement

Move up to your character's Speed. Most characters have a Speed of 30 feet. When using a grid to play, each space is 5 feet.

#### 1 Action

All characters get 1 Action per turn. Some characters use their weapons to take the Attack action, while others cast spells using the Magic action.

#### 1 Bonus Action

You only can take a Bonus Action if your character has an ability that costs a Bonus Action. The fighter class' Second Wind feature is an example of a Bonus Action ability.

#### Example Turn - Joe the Fighter

1. Joe uses 10 feet of **MOVEMENT** to get in range of the evil orc.
2. Joe uses his **ACTION** to attack the evil orc with his Shortbow.
3. Joe uses 20 feet of **MOVEMENT** to go the complete opposite direction.
4. Joe uses his **BONUS ACTION** to activate his Second Wind fighter feature, replenishing his health.

#### Reactions

During each round of combat, you also have one other resource: the **Reaction**.

Reactions are uncommon and less important. Generally your character will only use a Reaction if:

- A creature provokes an Opportunity Attack against you. This happens if an enemy moves out of your reach in combat without taking the Disengage action.
- You have a feature that lets you use a Reaction (e.g., the spell Counterspell can be cast as a Reaction).

### Out of Combat

When you are out of combat, the game does not follow a specific structure. Just listen to your DM, tell them what your character is doing, and collaborate with your fellow players to overcome challenges.

The rest is up to you and your group. These guidelines can be helpful for new players:

- Share the spotlight with the other players at your table.
- Be a team player.
- Don't get bogged down by making optimal choices—D\&D isn't a game about winning.
